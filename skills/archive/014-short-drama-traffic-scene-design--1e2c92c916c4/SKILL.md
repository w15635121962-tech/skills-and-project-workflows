# Short Drama Traffic Scene Design

Turn story material into a single readable “emotional event” before expanding it into a storyboard or AI-video prompt. Preserve the user's characters, world, safety limits, and established Format C template; this skill controls promotional dramaturgy, not camera-format replacement.

## Workflow

1. Extract the relationship, desired audience emotion, concrete object being contested, power imbalance, public witness, irreversible loss, and reversal clue.
2. Choose one primary archetype: public humiliation, intimate betrayal, forced choice, identity reversal, misunderstanding punishment, family favoritism, credit theft, forbidden tension, countdown danger, public truth exposure, return from presumed death, or bloodline awakening.
3. Build the event as `extreme situation + wrong choice + concrete cost + unfinished reversal`.
4. Convert abstract conflict into visible action and props: remove a ring, tear an agreement, close a door, seize an object, play evidence, block an exit, turn a crowd, reveal an insignia, or make danger visibly advance.
5. Stage power through height, seat, crowd density, isolation, camera/phone witnesses, and who controls the doorway or public screen. Make the reversal spatially readable through standing, opening a path, changing the central position, or moving the antagonist to the edge.
6. Select the duration structure below, then allocate a hook, pressure, victim reaction, cost evidence, reversal clue, and cut-point. Do not explain the reversal completely.
7. If the user requests video prompts, hand the event to the existing Format C/storyboard workflow. Keep each shot independently generatable and preserve axis, blocking, prop ownership, support plane, wardrobe, lighting, and reaction logic.

## Five-layer conflict test

- Relationship: can the viewer identify who is connected to whom immediately?
- Interest: what specific thing is being won, lost, withheld, or chosen?
- Power: who has the crowd, rules, money, family, status, evidence, or physical control?
- Irreversible cost: what concrete relationship, identity, opportunity, reputation, freedom, safety, or life change cannot simply be reset?
- Unfinished reversal: what person, document, signal, screen, symbol, door, or danger is about to change the audience's interpretation?

Reject scenes that are only people arguing. Add a visible event carrier and a settled consequence.

## Duration templates

- 15 seconds: 0–2s result/action hook; 2–6s relationship and contested object; 6–10s pressure or wrong choice; 10–13s reversal clue; 13–15s cut before the answer.
- 30 seconds: 0–3s result first; 3–10s relationship and motive; 10–18s public pressure or deprivation; 18–24s irreversible cost; 24–28s clue; 28–30s cliffhanger.
- 60 seconds: 0–5s visual hook; 5–15s relationship; 15–30s first humiliation/choice; 30–45s loss; 45–55s evidence or key arrival; 55–60s maximum reversal cut.

## Scene and shot requirements

- Lead with the primary emotion and a one-sentence event logline.
- State the relationship, contested object, power imbalance, irreversible cost, witness/public carrier, reversal clue, and exact cut-point.
- Use the chain: hook/relationship → pressure action → victim reaction → cost evidence → reversal clue → cliffhanger.
- Give every visible character readable attention shifts, physical choices, and settled endpoints. Silent characters still visibly react.
- Include at least one emotional close-up or action close-up with clear ownership of the face, hand, prop, or force consequence.
- Make the scene understandable with sound off and identify a strong cover frame.
- For adult romance or forbidden tension, use non-explicit, non-nude staging. For danger, medicine, crime, or disaster, avoid gratuitous gore and soften explicit injury when the platform is unknown.
- Do not invent dialogue, identities, twists, or moral judgments absent from the user's source unless asked to create them.

## Final audit

- Is there an action or strong result in the first three seconds?
- Can a silent viewer identify the victim, relationship, contested object, and power difference?
- Does the protagonist lose something concrete?
- Is there a witness, crowd, public screen, rule, or other emotional amplifier?
- Is the reversal clue visible before the final cut without giving away the answer?
- Can the concept be cut into both 15-second and 30-second variants?
- Is there a cover-worthy frame and a question that can provoke audience sides?

Default emotional arc: make the audience angry, then hurt, then hopeful; cut just before retaliation or truth becomes complete.
