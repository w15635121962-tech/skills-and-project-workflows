# Professional Cinematic Lighting Positions

When describing lighting, use these standard film lighting terms combined with their visual language and classic film references.

## Front-Side Key Light (前侧光)
Light source positioned at front-side of subject, combining front-light brightness with side-light dimensionality.

**Visual language**: Space and atmosphere creation, facial three-dimensional contour emphasis.
**Reference**: Andy Dufresne reading in prison by the window in *The Shawshank Redemption*.
**Best for**: Portraying resilient, intelligent, complex characters.

## Top Light / Overhead (顶光)
Light source directly from above (e.g., ceiling light).

**Visual language**: Oppression and intimidation, unnatural feeling, sci-fi/horror undertones.
**Reference**: Marlon Brando in *The Godfather Part I*, Hannibal Lecter in *The Silence of the Lambs*.
**Best for**: Interrogation scenes, power dynamics, psychological pressure, villain reveals.

## Side Light (正侧光)
Light source from the side, creating strong light/dark contrast (chiaroscuro).

**Visual language**: Three-dimensionality and texture, drama (complex personality or conflict).
**Reference**: Kane's close-up in *Citizen Kane*, *The Silence of the Lambs*.
**Best for**: Moral ambiguity, internal conflict, textured character portraits.

## Back Light / Rim Light (逆光 / 背光)
Light source directly behind subject, facing camera.

**Visual language**: Mystery and the unknown, solitude, hope and redemption, silhouette effect.
**Reference**: Opening of *Arrival*, character entrance in *Blade Runner*, Léon opening the door in *Léon: The Professional*.
**Best for**: Hero entrances, emotional revelation, silhouettes, spiritual moments.

## Butterfly / Paramount Light (蝴蝶光)
Light source at high position directly above camera, creating butterfly-shaped shadow under nose.

**Visual language**: Female elegance and nobility, enhanced drama and ritual, dreamy "goddess" imagery.
**Reference**: Vivien Leigh in *Gone with the Wind*, *The Great Gatsby*.
**Best for**: Beauty shots, glamour, formal portraiture, dream sequences.

## Front Light (顺光)
Light source from same direction as camera, directly illuminating subject.

**Visual language**: Objectivity and truth, bright positive atmosphere, documentary feel.
**Reference**: *Bicycle Thieves*.
**Best for**: Establishing realism, documentary-style sequences, neutral exposition.

## Bottom Light / Under Light (底光)
Light source from low position pointing upward.

**Visual language**: Villain/horror imagery, mental abnormality or psychological distortion.
**Reference**: *The Exorcist*, *The Shining*.
**Best for**: Horror, villain reveals, psychological terror, supernatural threats.

## Side-Back / 3/4 Back Light (侧逆光)
Light source positioned behind and to the side.

**Visual language**: Mystery and layering, emphasizing silhouette while partially hiding details, dynamic subject-background separation.
**Reference**: Mathilda in the office in *Léon: The Professional*, chase scenes in *The Bourne Identity*.
**Best for**: Action sequences, mysterious character introductions, dynamic separation.

## Prompt Integration

Always specify the light position explicitly in prompts:
- "front-side key light creating resilient atmosphere"
- "top light producing oppressive interrogation tension"
- "side-back light with snow reflection for dynamic action"

Include film references to elevate professionalism and emotional expression.
